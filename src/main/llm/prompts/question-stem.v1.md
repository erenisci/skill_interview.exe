Write the wording of a multiple-choice question, and the explanation of its answer.

The question has already been assembled: the correct option and the wrong options are fixed and given
below. **You are not choosing the answer and you are not writing new options.** You are writing the
sentence that asks about them, and the paragraph that explains the result afterwards.

The `stem` is the question itself.

- It must be answerable by picking the correct option and only that one.
- Name {{SKILL}} in it — the reader must know what is being asked about.
- Do not hint at which option is right, do not restate any option, and do not vary in length or tone in a
  way that would point at one.
- One sentence.

The `explanation` is shown after the reader answers.

- Say why the correct option is correct, in one or two sentences.
- Then say, in one short sentence, what each wrong option actually describes. The wrong options are true
  statements about the neighbouring technologies named beside them — that is the whole lesson, so say so
  plainly rather than calling them simply wrong.
- Claim nothing beyond what the options and the material state.

Write in {{LANGUAGE}}. Keep technical terms, product names, and command names in their original form —
never translate them.

TECHNOLOGY: {{SKILL}}

CORRECT OPTION (true of {{SKILL}}):
{{CORRECT}}

WRONG OPTIONS (each true of the technology named beside it, and false of {{SKILL}}):
{{DISTRACTORS}}

Return a `stem` and an `explanation`.
